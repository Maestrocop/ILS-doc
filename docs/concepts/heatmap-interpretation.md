# Heatmap Interpretation

The ILS heatmap is a visual summary of student mastery across outcomes.

## What the heatmap shows

- Rows represent outcomes.
- Columns represent students, classes, or cohorts.
- Each cell shows the mastery score for a student/outcome pair.

## Color and scoring

- Cells are colored to indicate performance bands.
- Common bands include low, medium, and high mastery.
- Colors make it easy to spot weak outcomes and struggling students.

## How teachers use it

- Identify outcomes with consistently low mastery.
- Spot students who have gaps across multiple outcomes.
- Detect patterns that suggest class-level instructional adjustments.

## Best practice

- Use the heatmap together with trend and at-risk signals.
- Look for clusters of low scores rather than reacting to individual cells.
- Compare heatmap rows and columns to identify whether the issue is outcome-specific or student-specific.

## Reading the map

- A row with many low cells suggests an outcome that needs instructional attention.
- A column with many low cells suggests a student who may need personalized support.
- Mixed colors across a row can indicate variable mastery for the same outcome.
- Mixed colors down a column can indicate a student who is inconsistent across outcomes.

## Next steps from the heatmap

- Use the heatmap to select target outcomes for intervention.
- Open the student progress detail view to inspect snapshot history and evidence.
- Combine heatmap findings with the cockpit and trend data for planning.
